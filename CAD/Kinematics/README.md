# NODI articulated mechanism — explicit joints and a solver run

![NODI motion generated by Bullet hinge and motor constraints](mechanism_motion.gif)

The original STEP is a static assembly. It does not contain editable revolute joints, and the earlier pose GIF was generated by assigned rotations. This supplement adds a native Blender assembly with **two actual Bullet hinge constraints**, **two angular motors**, and all **67 original CAD meshes** attached rigidly to three links. Body poses are calculated by the solver; only motor angular velocities are keyed.

**This demonstrates an ideal two-axis mechanism. It does not establish that the unbuilt hardware will move correctly.** In particular, the spline fit, horn grip, sleeve restraint, clearances under load, and wiring remain prototype verification items.

## Open and move the assembly

1. Download the complete repository ZIP and unzip it, or clone the repository.
2. Open [NODI_Articulated_RevA.blend](NODI_Articulated_RevA.blend) in Blender **4.5 LTS**. The native file embeds all component meshes.
3. Start at frame **1**, then press **Space** to calculate and play the 13-second motion test. Timeline markers identify yaw, pitch and software-limit tests. Rewind to frame 1 before replaying.
4. Inspect `Three ideal rigid links` and `Two hinges and two angular motors` in the outliner. The links have Bullet rigid-body properties; the empty objects have `HINGE` or `MOTOR` constraints. The mesh children retain their part IDs.
5. To inspect a specific frame reliably, play or step through the preceding frames first; the solver needs its previous state. The CSV and GIF are available without Blender.

The `.blend` is an editable **mesh assembly**, not a replacement for editable parametric CAD. [../build_nodi.py](../build_nodi.py) remains the source of the solid geometry and STEP exports. No FreeCAD/SolidWorks assembly file or native CAD joint solver is claimed.

## How the mechanism transmits motion

```text
Grounded base and pan servo case
    |
    +-- YAW revolute joint: Z axis through X=0, Y=0
          |
          +-- Pan horn, its retaining screw, yoke and pitch horn stay together
                |
                +-- PITCH revolute joint: local X through Y=0, Z=88 mm
                      |
                      +-- Pitch servo CASE, head shell, bezel and OLED stay together
```

**Yaw:** the pan servo case is fastened to the base cup. Its output drives the matched horn, which is attached to the yoke by two M2 screws. The yoke journal passes through the deck bore; a PTFE washer is intended to support thrust, with the actual load sharing still unverified. Turning the servo output turns the complete yoke and head around Z. The horn-to-output spline grip is assumed in this model.

**Pitch uses inverse mounting:** the pitch servo case is fastened inside the head. Its output horn is fastened to the yoke by two M2 screws and is therefore stationary relative to the yoke. Actuating the servo rotates the case and head relative to that held output. A brass sleeve and retained washer/bolt stack on the opposite side support the head on the same X axis. The full visual assembly preserves that distinction: the pitch horn belongs to the yoke link, while the pitch case belongs to the head link. Electrical pulse sign must be calibrated on the actual unit.

**In the GIF:** base cup, deck and head shell are hidden; the yoke is translucent so the horns can be seen. The moving pitch servo case is colored blue for identification. A render-only 0.01 mm outward display offset on the translucent yoke prevents coincident contact faces from creating visual artifacts. Rendering does not change the source solids or solver meshes. Floating screw heads indicate hardware whose surrounding shell is omitted in this cutaway.

The GIF samples the first 7.5 seconds of the 13-second solver run. The full limit tests are in the native scene and CSV. [Render provenance](render_provenance.json) records the exact displayed solver frames.

| Joint | Parent → child | Child-relative-parent axis | Operating limits | Physical stops, separate CAD audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yaw | Base → yoke | +Z at X=Y=0; representative point Z=34 mm | −30° to +30° | −35° to +35° |
| Pitch | Yoke → head | Local +X at Y=0, Z=88 mm | −15° to +25° | −20° to +30° |

There is one independent rotation per joint. The 67 mesh components have fixed link memberships, represented as explicit fixed joints in the URDF. The snooze button is treated as fixed for this mechanism study.

## What was actually calculated

[solver_validation.json](solver_validation.json) contains the numerical results; [solver_motion.csv](solver_motion.csv) contains all 780 frames. The native file was also reopened in a separate Blender process and replayed: three rigid links, four constraints, 67 meshes and approximately 24.95° yaw at frame 90 were confirmed.

| Executed check | Result |
|---|---|
| Solver | Blender 4.5.3 LTS, Bullet rigid-body constraint solver |
| Integration | 780 frames at 60 fps; 20 substeps/frame; 100 constraint iterations |
| Yaw, measured | Approximately −30.0004° to +30.0028° |
| Pitch, measured | Approximately −15.0207° to +25.0205° |
| Maximum hinge anchor separation | Below 0.00005 mm in the numerical model |
| Maximum hinge-axis misalignment | Below 0.0001° in the numerical model |
| Angular-limit acceptance | Both ends exercised; within 0.1° numerical tolerance |
| Independent URDF comparison | 52,260 component transforms checked across all frames |

These very small residuals describe **numerical constraint closure**. They are not manufacturing tolerances, predicted physical accuracy or backlash estimates. Small angular overshoot is a numerical solver effect.

The URDF audit parses the separate joint graph and computes forward kinematics using only Python's standard library. It compares every component's source-mesh transform with the recorded Bullet solution. [urdf_validation.json](urdf_validation.json) records the maximum difference: approximately 0.00011 mm at the mesh origins. Both the manifest and all input STL files are checked by SHA-256.

The independent [mechanism audit](../../Documentation/mechanism_audit.md) records the physical drive-path and retention concerns.

## Assumptions that still need hardware evidence

- **Horn/shaft torque coupling:** source CAD represents the purchased servo as an envelope and uses a smooth circular horn socket. Spline teeth and clamping/contact behavior are absent. The model idealizes a rigid grip; a matched commercial horn and sample interface inspection are required. A coincident smooth cylindrical surface is not proof of a torque-transmitting connection.
- **Pitch support:** the passive sleeve is treated as a fixed centerline support, with the head rotating on it. The nominal sleeve is OD4 × ID3.2 × 14.2 mm; both the head pivot and yoke use 4.2 mm clearance bores. Sleeve antirotation, clamping, axial restraint and bearing friction require prototype confirmation. The joint solver removes those clearance motions by assumption.
- **Servo internals:** commercial case and output envelopes are combined in the existing CAD. The output is axisymmetric; the separate rotor and gear train are not modeled. The explicit joints express the assumed relative case/output motion, while the mesh cannot display internal gear operation.
- **Dynamics:** gravity is zero. Inertias are bounding-box approximations at estimated centers of mass. Motor impulse is an arbitrary solver setting. This run supports no torque, acceleration, stall, fatigue or load-capacity claim.
- **Contact:** collisions are disabled between the three rigid links to isolate joint behavior. Hard-stop contact, physical interference, backlash and cable forces are not tested here. Existing B-rep collision sweeps remain a separate, discrete geometry audit.
- **Wires:** flexible harnesses are omitted. Service-loop length, bend radius, strain relief and endurance must be checked over combined yaw and pitch motion.

## Regenerate

From the repository root, after generating the original CAD exports:

```bash
blender --background --factory-startup --python CAD/Kinematics/build_articulated.py
python3 CAD/Kinematics/verify_articulated.py
blender --background --factory-startup --python CAD/Kinematics/render_solver_motion.py
python3 CAD/Kinematics/compose_solver_motion.py
```

The first command writes the native `.blend`, URDF, CSV, solver world matrices and validation JSON. The second independently audits the URDF. The third renders recorded solver matrices to `/tmp/nodi-bullet-frames`; it does not replace the constraint solver with assigned angular poses. The fourth requires Pillow and assembles the labeled cutaway GIF and still image.

[NODI_Articulated_RevA.urdf](NODI_Articulated_RevA.urdf) is a **kinematic visualization description** with two revolute and 67 fixed joints. It references `../exports/meshes/*.stl` and scales millimeters to meters. Keep the repository structure intact. Inertials, collision geometries and dynamic actuation are intentionally absent; its parser-required effort and velocity limits are zero. Configure verified physical properties and a controller before using it in a dynamic robot simulator. The executed solver evidence comes from Blender/Bullet, not a claimed ROS or Gazebo run.

Files in this folder deliberately distinguish the source joint model, executed numerical results and proposed physical tests. No FEA results are included.
